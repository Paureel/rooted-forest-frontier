## Semantic examination

The upper map at proof line 33 adds the single block `{alpha,beta,rho}`. Its span stops above the old tree root, so it cannot meet an old block span; after suppression its rooted restriction is the same in both inputs. The lower map at lines 35–39 uses two distinct facts: any mixed block reaches the old tree root, and a mixed block containing both markers would contain incompatible rooted triples. Together they force at least one block with no old label. Erasure therefore decreases component count by at least one. Applying both inequalities to a minimum forest gives the stated identity and witness optimality at line 41. These are my checks of the argument, not inferences from the finite tests or Lean build status.

Neither direction uses an ancestry-cycle condition. The direct-augmentation example at line 20 has reversed ancestor order for its two blocks, so its displayed two-block forest is expressly within ordinary AF semantics. I checked the example's four old-label triples by hand: the cherries are respectively `bc/ab`, `bd/ab`, `cd/ad`, and `cd/bd` for subsets `abc`, `abd`, `acd`, and `bcd`. Each pair differs. The two other two-plus-two partitions have overlapping spans, and adjoining rho to either block of `{a,b}|{c,d}` causes overlap in one input. I found no defect in the claimed direct-augmentation obstruction. This hand check does not independently verify the sentence about the finite oracle.

The unary-root and binary sister-leaf encodings have the same AF feasibility by the local correspondence described at line 53. A rho singleton occupies only its own vertex in either model. A nonsingleton rho block uses the same vertices of the unaugmented tree in both models; replacing the labelled unary root by an unlabeled root and a pendant rho leaf preserves restriction agreement. Old-only blocks are unchanged. This supports the written correspondence, but `Transfer.lean:14` defines only the binary model and contains no theorem proving the correspondence.

The formal limitation is explicit and substantial: `optimum_shift_from_maps` at `Transfer.lean:119–136` assumes the lift and erase maps at lines 127–128. Its conclusion is an arithmetic consequence of those assumptions and the two minimum specifications. The local disagreement and common-root lemmas do not construct finite partitions, prove the maps valid, or establish hereditary restriction. I found no mismatch between that limitation and the retained receipt.

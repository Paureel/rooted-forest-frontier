# Independent corridor derivation (recorded before proof reports)

Assume the exact geometry stated in SECOND-EXCHANGE-TARGET.md. Work with the rooted directed forest before yielding; each original arc has its own ID. Endpoint subtrees a,c and sibling subtree x are nonempty; F is reduced binary except for a labelled unary rho root. Let P be the a--c path, u its LCA, v its parent, x the other child of v. B is precisely the two pendant edges at the internal non-LCA vertices of P.

1. A non-LCA internal vertex of P has one child toward its endpoint and exactly one other child. The other child's edge belongs to B. The LCA u has its two children on P and has no off-path child. Thus exactly two non-LCA internal corridor vertices exist. Cutting B detaches two nonempty rooted subtrees. Their roots already have either zero or two children and survive. Every non-LCA interior corridor vertex becomes unary and suppresses. No other vertex loses a child, no component becomes label-empty, and u retains two nonempty child branches. Since v retains u and x, both v and x survive. This includes v an unlabelled component root; absence of an incoming edge stays absence. A rho ancestor and its incident edge are unaffected.

2. A suppression replaces two adjacent path edges by their concatenated path. Every affected non-B edge lies on P. Consequently every edge outside B union P appears in G=yield(F-B) with exactly its original ID and endpoints; G's newly compressed edges have provenance consisting solely of P edges. In particular ex=(v,x), eu=(v,u), and ev when present are unchanged. This is a literal edge claim, not merely a label-partition claim.

3. Let K contain B and avoid P, with C=K-B. All of C consists of the unchanged edges in point 2. Remove C from either raw F-B or G. All corridor arcs remain uncut. Hence the same two unary substitutions may still be performed in F-K: both original corridor vertices still have a parent and exactly one child, even if the endpoint branches later become label-empty. Each substitution preserves directed reachability between the remaining vertices and labels, including x,u,v. Discarding empty unlabelled vertices and other unary vertices afterward is exactly the usual yield operation. Thus yield(F-K)=yield(G-C) as labelled rooted forests. The substitutions establish an isomorphism before these later reductions, fixing x,u,v, so the sets of descendant labels still connected to x,u and the complete component label sets at these vertices agree even when one of those sets is empty. Costs satisfy |K|=|B|+|C|=2+|C| because B and C are disjoint actual original edges.

4. In G the x--u path has just ex and eu. A full second normalization removes its cut edges from C, then optionally inserts ev when both were cut and ev exists. It neither removes B (B is no longer in G) nor adds a compressed corridor arc. Thus C' is again a subset of unchanged original edges outside B union P. Apply point 3 to K'=B union C'. It gives literal lifting, equal yield and fragments, and |K'|=2+|C'|. An absent ev is never manufactured. No optimality or AF premise is used in this geometry lemma.

The implication from equal yields to AF membership requires the separate component normalization theorem in the appropriate current pair. The argument above alone proves neither that theorem nor a general cost-preserving suppression lift. It does not preserve identities of every original edge: P edges are precisely the exception. Later arbitrary compression and protected origins remain outside this derivation.

Potential failure sites to attack: unlabelled component-root variant, rho singleton variant, cuts emptying u/x endpoint fragments, cuts inside detached B subtrees, and deliberately retaining a suppressed P edge as unchanged.
